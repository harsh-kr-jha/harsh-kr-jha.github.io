The Idea

Tamaso brings fragmented operational data into one contextual model, a living map of how an organisation actually works.

From raw operations to action

  1. 01   Connect scattered systems and processes
  2. 02   Structure them into a unified operational model
  3. 03   Visualise the organisation as a digital twin
  4. 04   Understand patterns through analytics and AI
  5. 05   Act on problems, predictions, and opportunities

Data economy → Insight economy

The work begins on the ground: understanding what managers and operators need, how information moves, and where it disappears between ERPs, spreadsheets, machines, sensors, and manual processes.

The ambition is simple: help ordinary people use deep technology to do extraordinary things.

Beyond Manufacturing The same intelligence infrastructure can eventually help make sense of healthcare, governance, infrastructure, and other complex organisations.

Learning to Build
"I am not a product of my circumstances.
I am a product of my decisions." Stephen R. Covey

Growing up in Purnea, Bihar, ambition had to be self-generated. There was no startup culture, no network of mentors, no visible path. But there was a particular kind of drive that small towns give you, the one that asks what's possible beyond the horizon.

That drive carried me to IIT Bombay, where I didn't just study, I built. Products used by thousands. Communities that shaped campus life. Systems that outlasted my time there. And then to Hilti Group, where I touched something different: the intersection of industrial hardware, large-scale software, and real-world manufacturing.

Now, all of it is channelled into Tamaso, an intelligence layer for the physical world, starting with the people and factories that power India's manufacturing economy.

Earlier Chapter · Co-Founder

Faoud,
Factory on Cloud.

Before Tamaso, I co-founded Faoud to bring greater visibility and structure to Indian manufacturing. We built workflows for factory discovery, bid comparison, production tracking, quality control, and execution.

Working closely with factories made one thing clear: organisations do not only need more software. They need an intelligence system that understands how their data, people, machines, and processes fit together. That learning became part of the foundation for Tamaso.
